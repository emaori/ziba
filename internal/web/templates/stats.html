Tokens are counted as the provider reported them, not estimated. Input and output are kept apart because they are priced apart — output costs several times more per token, so a single total would hide the figure that moves the bill.

The interest rows count an article once for each interest it belongs to, so they add up to more than the overall total: an article filed under two subjects cost something to both. The day is the day the model ran, not the day the article was collected — the two tables above group by collection, and a backfill spends every token in one afternoon on articles gathered over weeks. Articles analyzed offline count as zero, which is what they cost.

Collected counts what reached the database. It is not what the sources offered: an item published before its source's cutoff, and a newsletter link filtered out as navigation, are both discarded before anything is written and leave nothing to count. One feed offers 277 entries and contributes three. Those rejections appear in the run's log only.
